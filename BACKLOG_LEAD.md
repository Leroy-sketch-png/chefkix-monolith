## LEAD v316 — Screen the 2015 Shino-lineage method and bound its small cooking evaluation (2026-10-15)

- **Text/provenance:** screened the rendered full text of Yamanishi et al., “Alternative-ingredient Recommendation Based on Co-occurrence Relation on Recipe Database,” *Procedia Computer Science* 60 (2015), 986–993, DOI [`10.1016/j.procs.2015.08.138`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2015.08.138), through its [ResearchGate public-full-text page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281412809_Alternative-ingredient_Recommendation_Based_on_Co-occurrence_Relation_on_Recipe_Database); cross-checked title, venue, DOI, and open-access status against the [Elsevier record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877050915022656). The shared page displays the paper's CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 notice and printed pages. Its PDF-download route returned 403, so the text was screened in-browser but no exact PDF bytes were retained or hashed; record this as a rendered-text screen, not a hash-verified acquisition.
- **Method/data:** §3 uses 4,508 randomly selected recipes from an 18,650-recipe “Kyou no Ryori”/NHK recipe site corpus, with a subjective seasoning dictionary to exclude seasoners; this is not a Cookpad corpus. Algorithm 1 ranks compatibility with the recipe's remaining ingredients using a Naive-Bayes-style co-occurrence score and explicitly does not use the exchanged ingredient. Algorithm 2 represents an ingredient's role by mutual-information relations to the remaining ingredients and ranks candidates by Hamming distance. The authors acknowledge that the method does not filter ingredient types: pork and clams appear among burdock alternatives; ingredient categorization is future work. The paper's allergy language is motivation only: its target cases were selected because preferences differed, no allergen constraint was tested, and allergy/nutrient factors for a hospital-diet system are future work.
- **Evaluation denominator / ceiling:** the paper cooks 12 selected replacements across two recipes (5 Chikuzen-ni/burdock and 7 green-curry-risotto/grape-tomato candidates); one author cooked them, and five subjects rated them. Table 5 contains 35 Algorithm-1 ratings and 25 Algorithm-2 ratings: A/B/C (the paper's acceptable group) totals 32/35 = 91.4% and 23/25 = 92.0%, respectively, or 55/60 = 91.7% combined. This reconciles the authors' “over 90% acceptable” statement to its actual rating denominator. It is not 90% of recipe pairs, a held-out benchmark, an independent replication, a blinded/calibrated judge study, or allergen/clinical validation; the paper leaves comparison with collaborative filtering as future work.
- **Lineage/disposition:** the 2015 text provides direct prior art for co-occurrence-based substitute ranking with recipe context. The related 2016 record adds ingredient-category/ontology wording, and the Springer 2018 chapter abstract reports that combination and an 88% figure, but exact data, cases, evaluator, and version relationship remain unverified. Keep the records distinct and do not count them as independent replications. This satisfies v315's 2015 text-screening action only at rendered-text level; retain the exact-byte/hash gap. ## LEAD v313 — Full-text screen of Shino et al.'s multi-aspect substitution method (2026-10-15)

- **Method and data boundary:** the article proposes three separate recommendation views: affinity to the recipe's remaining ingredients (recipe co-occurrence with a Naive-Bayes-style score), role similarity (word2vec over ingredient-list order/co-occurrence), and texture similarity (word2vec over ingredient names and texture terms in recipe reviews). Allergy appears as motivation/context, not a tested safety outcome.
