# §12 nutrient-observation source admission — 2026-11-23

## Lead decision

**Adopt USDA FoodData Central (FDC) Foundation Foods, October 2026 JSON, as the sole initial source/data-type candidate for a separately versioned, source-native food-to-nutrient-observation component in `graph_product_v4`.** The official downloads page lists that release. USDA's FDC API Guide says FDC data are public-domain/CC0 and asks users to cite FDC, so permission to use FDC data itself is not an open blocker. This decision is not evidence that the specific archive bytes were acquired, hash-pinned, or materialized; its exact files/schema still need verification before M-A processes them. CC0 does not grant permission for unrelated non-FDC material that might later be combined with it. No source rows were accessed for this decision.

Keep this component out of the frozen train-derived `graph_eval_v4`. It is not a nutrient-equivalence graph, health/safety/allergen result, recipe-context measure, or product recommendation. FooDB `Content`/`Nutrient` presence counts in the T504 crosswalk remain a bounded presence diagnostic; they are not nutrient amounts, a nutrient profile, or an accepted ingredient-nutrient relation. This is consistent with the existing §12 rule that nutrient relations need their own versioned, rights-permitted input and identity/completeness semantics.

## Representation and identity rules

1. Represent each source-native nutrient value as a versioned observation tied to its exact FDC food record and FDC nutrient ID—not as a bare, cross-source `Ingredient→Nutrient` fact. Preserve the FDC data type and release, `fdc_id`, any distinct FDC Source ID/sample identity, `food_nutrient.id`, `nutrient_id`, source/derivation identifier, and exact source provenance. FDC assigns an FDC ID to a food record and a Source ID to a unique sample; these identities are not interchangeable.
2. Preserve the reported quantity and unit, basis (including per-100-g or per-100-ml), food form/state, edible/refuse/portion qualifiers, derivation/source, and available `data_points`, `standard_error`, `min`, `max`, `median`, footnote, acquisition/date, and sample metadata. Missing optional statistics remain missing/unknown. Do not convert units, portion bases, or forms unless a separately versioned transformation records the exact conversion and inputs.
3. Do not collapse records by display name. Raw, cooked, dried, drained, enriched, edible-part, or other distinct source-described states stay distinct FDC records unless a later reviewed mapping explicitly relates them. Foundation documentation notes that some agricultural records may be shown on a 0%-moisture basis rather than as sold; preserve that basis rather than labeling every record simply “edible per 100 g.”
4. A missing nutrient row/value means **UNKNOWN / not supplied**, not zero and not ABSENT. FDC itself says some nutrients are unanalysed while others may not occur in a food; absent rows alone cannot distinguish those. ABSENT requires an explicit source-declared completeness contract for the exact food record, nutrient universe, release, form and method scope.
5. Preserve numeric zero as a reported source value, not a proof of biological absence. For Foundation Foods, where an LOQ field indicates a below-quantification result, preserve both the stored value and LOQ and represent it as censored/below-LOQ; where the older/source record lacks LOQ, record that censoring status as unknown. Never replace `<LOQ` with a true zero. For any future Branded lane, label-rounded zero and “not a significant source” are label statements, not analytically established absence.
6. Do not average, select, or treat conflicting observations across records, forms, releases, or data types as one truth. Retain each value and its exact provenance; any derived comparison/aggregation needs a separate frozen method and must identify its included observations and exclusions.
7. FDC nutrient IDs are source-qualified identities. A nutrient name is display text, not a cross-database identifier. Any mapping to an external nutrient vocabulary or to a ChefKix ingredient ID must be a separately versioned, reviewed mapping with provenance; no fuzzy-name join is accepted.
8. M-A's source-native observation component remains separate from M-B's ChefKix ingredient bridge. M-B must map an exact FDC food record/state to a canonical ingredient ID using reviewed mapping-level evidence; ambiguity/unmatched cases remain unresolved and are not attached to ingredient nodes. This does not change the already adopted FooDB Food→Compound predicate or the frozen substitution/evaluation dataset.

## Official source documentation checked

- [FDC data type comparison and documentation index](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation/) — distinct purposes and source approaches for Foundation, Experimental, FNDDS, Branded, and SR Legacy.
- [FDC downloadable releases](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/) — Foundation Foods October 2026 JSON listed; the page notes the CSV issue was corrected on 2026-11-19. No archive was downloaded or hash-checked here.
- [Foundation Foods documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/Foundation_Foods_Documentation/) — individual samples, source/derivation context, food forms, basis/portion caveats, incomplete nutrient coverage, and LOQ semantics.
- [FDC Download Field Descriptions](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/portal-data/external/dataDictionary) — `food_nutrient.amount` is per 100 g in the nutrient table's unit; fields include `fdc_id`, `nutrient_id`, `data_points`, `derivation_id`, `standard_error`, `min`, `max`, `median`, and `footnote`; nutrient records define unit and nutrient identifier.
- [FDC Help: IDs, values, and LOQ](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/help/) — food-record and sample IDs, data-type-specific food codes, summary-value definitions, and LOQ.
- [FDC API Guide — Licensing](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/) — USDA says FDC data are public domain/CC0, permission is not needed for their use, and requests FDC attribution. This statement is scoped to FDC data, not other sources or artifacts.
- [FDC Branded Foods documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/GBFPD_Documentation/) — partner-supplied label information, per-100-unit standardization, derivation, missing values, and label-rounded zero semantics.

These source definitions were checked on 2026-11-23. The documentation supports this bounded design decision; it does not substitute for exact release-file verification or operation-specific rights review.
