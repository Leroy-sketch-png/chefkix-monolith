## Source-backed method and data change

Rita et al., *Optimizing Ingredient Substitution Using Large Language Models to Enhance Phytochemical Content in Recipes*, arXiv:2409.08792 (v1, 13 September 2024), subsequently published in *Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction* 6(4), 2738–2752 (2024), DOI `10.3390/make6040131`.

- **Selection and exclusions:** The paper applies GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106 to Recipe1MSubs records, with the associated recipe context, asking whether the proposed substitute is feasible in that recipe. It assigns `Correct`, `Potential`, or `Incorrect`; the filtered data retain `Correct` and remove `Potential` and `Incorrect`. The stated request settings include temperature 0.5, at most 10 output tokens, and five runs. The operation is corpus filtering before model training/evaluation, not a per-user selective-prediction or abstention rule.
- **Labels and identity:** These are GPT-generated suitability categories, not independent human judgments. For Hit@1, the paper lowercases names, removes numeric values, applies character normalization, then clusters similar ingredient names (including examples such as rice varieties); a top prediction counts when it matches the recorded substitute or its cluster. This is not an exact-identity or blinded human A/C/N/U evaluation.
- **Splits and denominators:** The reported original Recipe1MSubs counts are 70,520 total: 49,044 train, 10,729 validation, 10,747 test. Filtering is reported over all three partitions. Across five filtered runs, mean retained counts are 31,819 train, 7,094 validation, and 7,085 test (45,998 total). One randomly selected filtered realization used for final modeling contains 44,615 rows: 31,063 train, 6,831 validation, 6,721 test. Thus the test denominator changes from 10,747 to 6,721; the filtered test cohort is not the same complete test cohort as the unfiltered comparison.
- **Reported results:** The authors report GISMo Hit@1 of 34.53±0.10% on original and 40.24±0.36% on filtered data (+5.71 percentage points), and fine-tuned GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106 of 38.03±0.28% and 54.46±0.29% (+16.43 pp), respectively. These are the authors' reported results, not reproduced here. Training data also change, and the filtered test set is selected by the same GPT-based validity filter; therefore the contrast does not isolate a training-data effect or constitute a same-case matched estimate. Do not transfer these values to ChefKix or use them as a cross-paper ranking.

## Leakage and validity boundary

The paper says it starts from the published train/validation/test partition and reports filtered counts within each partition. Applying a GPT suitability filter to every partition creates **test-set selection/conditioning**: the reported filtered-set score is conditional on which test substitutions that filter retained. This is a concrete comparability risk, especially because the filter and one evaluated model share the GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106 model family. It is **not enough evidence to assert conventional train/test leakage**: the paper does not establish cross-partition recipe or pair overlap, whether filtering decisions or responses were reused across partitions, or row-level identity/disjointness. Its claim of “almost perfect ingredient substitute similarity” across partitions is not operationalized sufficiently here to resolve those questions. Preserve the leakage/overlap conclusion as `UNKNOWN`, not as either clean independence or proven contamination.

The reported improvement supports only this bounded proposition: under the authors' GPT-filtered Recipe1MSubs evaluation design, their re-trained systems obtained higher reported Hit@1 on the filtered cohort than on the original cohort. Because both training material and evaluation membership changed, it does not by itself show that refinement caused the increase, that predictions are more acceptable to cooks, or that the effect would hold on a fixed untouched cohort.

## L42 / C1–C3 disposition

- **C1:** S03 is direct prior art for automated recipe-context substitution-validity filtering and Hit@1 evaluation on Recipe1MSubs. It narrows any broad claim that this kind of filtering/evaluation is absent. It does not report the proposed independent human per-candidate culinary-function A/C/N/U audit, blind shared-candidate comparison, exact-gold versus any-acceptable human estimand, or identity-stratified analysis. Those differences may be described as design distinctions, not as established novelty; exact-design novelty remains `UNKNOWN`/`UNESTABLISHED` pending the wider funnel and qualified review.
- **C2:** S03 is a relevant comparator and a warning to freeze case identity, normalization, and denominator. Its original-versus-filtered figures are not a matched comparison on unchanged test cases and do not validate a ChefKix C2 arm or its performance. The existing matched-cohort requirements remain appropriate.
- **C3:** The paper concerns dataset-level curation before retraining. It is adjacent to data-quality filtering but does not establish a calibrated per-query allergen filter, tri-state evidence handling, safe-set guarantee, or abstention criterion.
- **Decision:** Replace “refinement definition unread” in the local SSoT summary with the bounded method and changed-test-denominator statement above. Keep the L42 question and full funnel open. No protocol, experiment, claims-register status, contribution acceptance, or milestone changes.

## Counter-reading, falsifier, and review trigger

The strongest counter-reading is that, because the original partition is retained and the model is evaluated on a filtered subset within each partition, the filtered results are a legitimate conditional benchmark rather than an invalid test. That reading is compatible with the evidence; it does not make the original-versus-filtered contrast matched or identify the cause of the gain. A versioned source/data artifact demonstrating that the reported cross-condition estimates were actually computed on a predeclared shared test intersection, with row-level paired predictions, would support a distinct matched comparison; it would not erase the paper's reported filtered-cohort design. Reopen only if a corrected article/version or source artifact changes the filtering, split, or evaluation procedure. The disposition is reversible and leaves the experiment untouched.
