## 3.1 Data are not interchangeable evidence

The study draws on several artifacts with different units and roles: substitution-pair rows, recipes, ranking vocabularies, graph edges, food-profile records, and runtime API responses. A row count in one is not a count of unique pairs in another; a node or profile count is not a coverage rate; and an API response is not proof of its producer or source lineage. Every result must name the exact file/version, identity rule, split, denominator, and transformation that produced it.

The recorded Recipe1MSubs/GISMo split contains 49,044 training, 10,729 validation, and 10,747 test rows, with 6,653 ranking candidates in the recorded identity inventory. The current strategy records CC BY-NC 4.0 for this source, while the Senath CSV has no license recorded in the project inventory; that is not a permission determination. Do not redistribute or upload source data based solely on local availability. Senath and GISMo are reported to use the same split, so their historical evaluations are not independent datasets.

MISKG's historical inventory records 80,044 rows, 39,194 unique directed pairs, 7,922 raw labels, an 8,552-entry unified vocabulary, and 36,763 normalized edges in one recorded version. These are different objects and version-specific counts, not one reconciled “dataset size.” Any new graph build must bind the exact source, transformation, and split rather than inherit these values by name.

Other sources have narrower roles. The installed bounded FooDB service is recorded as 971 food profiles, 206 named dimensions, and 125,992 binary links; a separate research fingerprint inventory has 975 entries. A later source-level check establishes a bounded Food→Compound presence relation for the ID-bearing 971-profile candidate, but does not turn either profile inventory into a complete graph or an accepted runtime artifact (details in §3.4 and the linked LEAD decision). FlavorDB/FlavorDB2 counts and associated PubChem identifiers vary by artifact and headline definition, and FlavorDB/FlavorGraph are correlated releases rather than two independent evidence families. RecipeNLG's 2,231,142 rows were used in a separate historical presence-overlap analysis; its source field is not a verified cuisine label and its recorded rights are non-commercial research/education. These inventories cannot be combined into a generic, fully licensed, semantically harmonized food graph.

## 3.3 Split roles and leakage boundaries

The strategy distinguishes an evaluation graph, `graph_eval_v4`, from a product graph, `graph_product_v4`. The former is constrained to train-derived substitution evidence; the latter may use rights-permitted sources but must never score held-out evaluation. Recipe rows, train-derived pair evidence, validation/test labels, and external descriptive profiles therefore need explicit lineage and split labels. A held-out pair can overlap a train pair under the intended pair-novelty analysis; that does not authorize using held-out labels to construct the evaluation graph. The unresolved PR11 wording conflict—row/source provenance versus pair exclusion—must be settled before the affected protocol is frozen.

For any report, define whether `seen` means exact row, exact identity pair, or another relation, and state the ingredient identity mapping before counting. Build the held-out frame and identity strata locally; inference payloads contain source-only fields and candidate pools, never gold labels, target-derived strata, or evaluation partitions. The recorded 108-shard full-split input preparation had exact coverage and fresh-process hash/join checks with labels excluded, but it is preparation—not upload authorization, protocol freeze, inference, or evaluation.

Coverage is endpoint- and pair-dependent. For the recorded full test split, 260/2,650 unique endpoint identities (9.81%) and 6,709/21,494 endpoint occurrences (31.21%) resolve to FooDB profiles; both endpoints resolve in 1,162/10,747 substitution rows (10.81%). Report each denominator. Partial profile coverage must remain separate from no profile, and neither state entails chemical absence. On the same train/test frame, GISMo source pools cover 10,747 cases and 537,350 candidate entries; that completeness is a pool-generation contract, not human-valid substitution coverage.

## 3.4 “Graph” denotes multiple artifacts

The historical R0 claims of a 25-node/59-edge graph and a 195,600-node/4,591-edge graph are unreconciled source assertions. They must not be blended with any of the more recent observations below.

A separate bounded FooDB source-relation check resolves one narrower identity question. The ID-bearing 971-profile candidate was joined to a hash-pinned FooDB 1.0 archive using `food_public_id → Food.public_id` only to resolve the profile, then `Content.food_id → Food.id` and typed `Content.source_type=Compound`, `Content.source_id → Compound.id` to establish each edge. All 125,992 candidate pairs matched that typed source relation; all 206 compound IDs fall within the converter's top-255 scope. This supports a private, bounded native edge predicate with source-native `Food.id` and `Compound.id` endpoints—not an edge reconstructed from normalized profile names. The official schema identifies internal `Food.id` as the `Content.food_id` join target ([FooDB schema](https://foodb.ca/schema)). Of the source-supported pairs, 107,496 pass the existing `Content.export == 1` plus public-Food exposure predicate; 18,496 do not. That operational filter is not itself a legal determination, and failed exposure eligibility must not be relabeled as source absence. The selected top-255 scope excludes 49 dangling IDs and four source Food records without named compounds; it is not the complete FooDB compound universe. The finding accepts source support only: the private graph still requires a deterministic build, manifest/output validation, and its own consumer/runtime evidence. The aggregate-only decision and owner boundary are recorded in [`BACKLOG_LEAD.md`](../../../BACKLOG_LEAD.md#lead-work-and-member-execution).

The serialized AI-service `substitution_subgraph.pt` was locally inspected under restricted deserialization in the recorded LEAD v397 census. ## 3.5 Rights and identity limits

Source inventories are not data-use approvals. The active strategy records unresolved rights or redistribution questions for several third-party datasets and profiles. Keep raw rights-limited data local, do not add it to a package or GPU upload by default, and make only permitted derived metadata portable. Exact-name joins are reproducible but incomplete; fuzzy or semantic mappings need an independently reviewed policy and a sensitivity track. An apparent missing source record is “unresolved in this artifact,” not evidence of absence from the world.

The resulting data story is not that a single complete culinary knowledge graph has been validated. It is a set of versioned, partial sources whose identities, coverage, graph constructions, and runtime activation have different evidence ceilings. This limitation is methodological: it affects what “seen,” “covered,” and “supported” mean throughout the thesis.

## 3.6 Reporting and next gates

Before publication, reconstruct the exact source/build manifests for all cited counts, confirm local artifact hashes against the recorded evidence receipts, verify the graph-layer descriptions against their source files and captured runtime, resolve PR11, and complete source-specific rights review. The claims-register seed is not a completed register; structural validation does not reproduce its claims. Do not replace any of the above with a single coverage percentage or graph-size headline.

**Supported at the recorded boundary:** specific split sizes and identity counts; the bounded T535 identity-presence/partition comparison in its exact pinned unfiltered artifact; deterministic source-only candidate-pool construction; exact-name linkage and profile coverage under stated rules; the bounded FooDB Food→Compound source relation within its declared top-255 candidate scope; a restricted local serialized-artifact census; and a dated local API response census. These do not jointly constitute one graph or prove that the FooDB relation is built or served.

**Not supported:** a single reconciled graph across artifacts; complete semantic identity; source-to-runtime producer provenance; redistribution permission; graph-based improvement; culinary validity; food safety; human usefulness; or deployed/product-ready behavior.
